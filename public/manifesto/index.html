The Open Mic Manifesto

Break Barriers. Take the Mic.

This manifesto sets out what CISO Open Mic stands for, what we ask of one another, and how we build an experience worth contributing to. It guides whom we bring together, how we participate, and the relationships we develop as a community.

These principles matter because honest conversation depends on trust. That trust grows when people contribute generously, respect different perspectives, and take responsibility for the experience they help create.

Security leadership means making difficult decisions with incomplete information and carrying responsibilities that often follow us home. Our experiences can help others—if we are willing to share them.

CISO Open Mic brings people together to speak honestly, listen carefully, and learn from one another.

Bring something real.

Share a difficult decision, a lesson learned, an assumption challenged, or a question you are still working through. You do not need every answer to make a valuable contribution. Honesty about uncertainty gives others permission to explore their own.

Make five minutes matter.

Five minutes. No slides. No sales pitches. Choose a point worth sharing, explain why it matters, and respect the time of those listening and waiting to speak.

The conversation may continue beyond your five minutes. When time allows, judges, the MC, and fellow participants can ask questions, challenge ideas, and explore different perspectives. Come ready to engage with curiosity and openness.

Welcome different voices.

We value diversity across genders, races, ethnicities, cultures, ages, abilities, backgrounds, and career paths. Different lived experiences strengthen the conversation. Help people feel welcome, heard, and able to contribute.

Security reaches across organizations and disciplines. CISOs, emerging leaders, and peers in technology, risk, privacy, governance, and business belong in the conversation, alongside researchers, advisors, partners, and other relevant stakeholders.

Come ready to give.

This community depends on reciprocity. Contribute experience, curiosity, encouragement, introductions, or time. Thoughtful listening and helping someone feel welcome count too.

Contribute in a spirit of fairness and generosity, with the community’s interests at heart. Helping others, making introductions, and offering support should come without an expectation of personal financial reward. Value what others contribute and help build something everyone can benefit from.

People who come only to take—whether contacts, opportunities, attention, food, or drinks—without contributing to others are not welcome. Hospitality is part of the experience, and accepting it carries a responsibility to help make that experience worthwhile.

Willingness to contribute guides whom we invite and whose requests to participate we accept.

Respect the care that goes into the room.

We curate each gathering around relevant experience, a balance of perspectives, willingness to contribute, and available space. We cannot include everyone every time. Participating once does not guarantee a future place, and no one is expected to join every experience we offer.

We may decline requests to participate, speak, or sponsor based on capacity, relevance, contribution, or conduct. We make those decisions respectfully, upholding both diversity and the standards of the community.

Build trust through transparency.

Participants must accurately identify their organization and role so we can validate who they are and understand what they bring. Those working independently or between roles must be equally clear about their circumstances.

We need this information to curate responsibly. Its purpose is to establish and preserve trust, and we cannot build the room without it.

Help future leaders grow.

Sharing experience, coaching, encouragement, and honest feedback are part of our commitment to developing future leaders.

Places for people early in their careers or still building the requisite experience are limited. Each gathering needs a strong foundation of experienced leaders to sustain the depth of conversation. We consider readiness, relevant experience, and willingness to contribute when deciding whom to include.

Those admitted on this basis must be accompanied by an experienced community member who supports them, especially the first time. That means preparing them, making introductions, and helping them participate. Inviting or accepting someone and leaving them to figure out the room alone is not enough.

Listen generously. Disagree respectfully.

Give people your attention. Ask questions that deepen understanding. Be prepared to reconsider your assumptions.

Different experiences will lead to different conclusions. Challenge the reasoning while treating the person with care.

Protect the trust that makes candour possible.

Share only what you are entitled to share. Carry lessons forward without identifying a contributor or their organization, directly or indirectly, unless they have explicitly permitted it. Being seen in the room does not give others permission to attribute your words.

Value the sponsors who make it possible.

Without sponsorship, CISO Open Mic cannot happen. Sponsors provide the resources that support meaningful conversations, connections, shared learning, and the development of future leaders. They deserve appreciation, respect, and thoughtful engagement, with no obligation to buy.

Sponsors join a collaborative, non-competitive space. We expect mutual respect and a shared commitment to the community, free from competitive positioning, sales pressure, and rivalry.

Enjoy the experience. Carry it forward.

Speakers, listeners, judges, organizers, volunteers, sponsors, and partners all help create a room worth being part of. Make space for humour, warmth, and the relief of finding people who understand.

Then follow up. Continue the discussion. Put an idea to work. Share what you learn. The value grows through what we do afterwards.

Bring your experience. Bring your curiosity. Help someone else find their voice.

Break Barriers. Take the Mic.
