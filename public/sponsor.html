The exact combination depends on the communities, events and objectives agreed with each sponsor.

✓

Logo, company overview and a link from the CISO Open Mic Sponsors page.

✓
Event and city recognition

Appropriate acknowledgement connected to the communities and gatherings being supported.

✓
Representative participation

Attendance opportunities for approved sponsor representatives within the agreed event scope.

✓
Agreed communications visibility

Recognition in relevant digital or event communications where it fits the relationship.

✓
Relevant professional information

Limited registration details may be shared only for an event the sponsor directly supports, where disclosed and permitted.

✓
Clear event preparation

A planning conversation covering logistics, participation expectations, community standards and the boundaries of the room.

These are possible components, not a universal package or guarantee. Every relationship is confirmed through an agreed scope before participation.

Protecting what makes it work

Visibility without taking over the room.

Sponsors help create the conditions for the community to gather. The community, local team and judges remain responsible for what happens inside it.

  • No sales pitches, product demonstrations or guaranteed time at the mic.
  • No control over programming, topic selection, judging, awards or participant approval.
  • No guaranteed leads, meetings, business outcomes or access to confidential conversations.
  • No attribution of participant comments that would violate the Chatham House Rule.
  • No commissions, referral fees or kickbacks tied to business that may follow an event.

Is there a fit?

The strongest relationships start with aligned expectations.

We are deliberate about who enters the room and equally deliberate about the organizations associated with it.

A strong fit looks like

  • Supporting practitioner-led communities for the long term.
  • Respecting a peer environment without pushing a sales agenda.
  • Investing in specific cities or regional relationships.
  • Valuing credibility, listening and meaningful professional connection.

It is probably not a fit if you expect

  • Pay-to-speak access or a guaranteed platform.
  • Control over invitations, judging, awards or topics.
  • An unrestricted attendee list or automatic lead generation.
  • A one-event trial before deciding whether to sponsor.
